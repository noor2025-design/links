# Hong Kong
## Project Overview
This website was created as part of the Links project for Typography and Interaction class. A core focus of this project was learning how to integrate APIs and work with a content management system while designing across multiple media types. The website pulls structured content and translates it into dynamic layouts that inlcudes images, video, audio, PDFs and text.  

It is a digital interpretation of an Are.na channel curated by a classmate focused on life, culture and history in Hong Kong. The channel brought together archival imagery, video, infrastructure, sound and written material that collectively portray the density, rhythm and identity of the city.

I approached the channel as a research archive and my goal was to design the content as an immersive digital experience that captures both the speed and memory embedded in Hong Kong.  

## Concept
After reviewing the collection, two themes became central to my design direction which were Vintage/Retro and Fast-paced Density. Many of the images carried an archival, cinematic quality. Hong Kong is globally known for its cinema, tightly packed urban environment and vertical architecture. I chose vintage retro aesthetic to reflect this visual history while using subtle interaction to communicate movement and energy. The design balances texture and motion honoring the past while presenting it in a contemporary web format. 
## Visual Language (Typography & Color)
Typography and color were central to reinforcing the atmosphere of the site. I chose typefaces that feel reminiscent of Hong Kong’s visual culture bold, condensed forms that echo cinema posters and urban signage. The typography carries a retro presence while still functioning clearly within a web layout.

The color palette is rich and deep, drawing from the tones often seen in Hong Kong of saturated reds, warm yellows, dark backgrounds, and high-contrast highlights. These colors reflect the city’s neon glow, dense architecture, and cinematic history. Together, the typography and palette create a visual identity that feels immersive.
## Structure & Experience
When the page loads, users are immersed in a background image layered with a transparent overlay and time-lapsed video. This established the energy and rhythm of the city immediately. Navigation at the top allows users to move between sections easily.

### Images Section
The first section presents still imagery from the collection. Each section includes Cantonese text to ground the experience culturally. Images feature a grain overlay to reinforce the archival tone. On hover, images subtly scale and reveal the name of the image, adding interaction without disrupting the cinematic atmosphere.
### Video Section
The video section is designed to resemble a vintage movie screening. A featured film is displayed prominently, with additional titles below, similar to a cinema lineup. Initially, videos appear as thumbnails. On hover, they can be played directly on the page, mimicking the experience of previewing a film. This interaction references Hong Kong’s strong cinematic identity.
### Audio Section 
The audio section includes music and ambient sounds from Hong Kong. This expands the experience beyond visuals. Sound represents the movement, density, and everyday life within the city.
### Text & Archive Section
The final section includes a quote about Hong Kong, a PDF, and longer written articles from the channel. This portion slows the experience down and encourages reflection after the visual and auditory immersion.
